session: hold background job output between commands

Output that a background job (`cmd &`) wrote while no command was running
was never shown. Nothing read the PTY between commands, so the bytes sat
in the kernel until the next run() read them and threw them away. A job
that wrote more than the PTY buffer holds blocked until the user ran
another command.

The session now keeps reading the PTY between commands on a thread of its
own. Once bash is seen waiting for its next line, what arrives is
background output: it goes into a 64 KiB buffer that keeps the newest
bytes and counts the dropped ones. It is never written to the terminal
and never becomes part of a CommandResult. The caller can ask whether
output is waiting (Session.background_waiting) and take it as one block
under a "[background output]" line, with an "[N bytes dropped]" line when
the buffer overflowed (Session.take_background_output). The bytes are
untrusted (ADR-0016).

The prompt and PROMPT_COMMAND output are still dropped as before. Job
notices that bash prints during a later command stay that command's
output. The reader stops before every run() and before close(). It holds
the session only through a weak reference, so a session dropped without
close() is still collected and its bash still exits.

pty-protocol v1 section 6.6 gains the exact boundary of what counts as
background output, and the conformance paragraph and threat model now say
what this covers. The notice during a confirm (#74) and showing the block
above the prompt (#78) build on this. This follows the terminal ownership
work in 59988582859b1003c8c9cc9467d5ae09257f6fcc.
